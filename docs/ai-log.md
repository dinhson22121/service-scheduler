## What I kept for myself

- Every scope and architecture decision (scenario, layer, extras, no Kafka or Redis, rate limiting at the single edge rather than inside the replicas).
- Acceptance by evidence: a green test run, the logs of both replicas, and metrics split across replicas. The AI saying "done" was never enough.
- Treating a passing test with suspicion when it could not have failed (step 11).

## Review notes

A separate reviewer agent made three findings. I checked each against the code and the libraries before acting.

| Finding | Check | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| HIGH: Postgres `53300 too_many_connections` returns 500, not 503 | Read `sql-error-codes.xml` in spring-jdbc: 53xxx maps to `DataAccessResourceFailureException`, which the handler did not catch. `CannotGetJdbcConnectionException` turned out to be a subclass of it | **Fixed**: the handler catches the superclass (pool timeouts and 53xxx) and returns 503 + Retry-After |
| MEDIUM: worst-case booking latency (retries × 2 s lock waits) can exceed nginx's 10 s read timeout | Part of the reasoning was wrong: nginx does **not** replay a `POST` on another replica after a timeout (`non_idempotent` is not set). The latency bound was a fair point | **Fixed**: `lock_timeout` lowered to 500 ms; lock holders keep it for milliseconds |
| LOW: an unexpected integrity violation becomes a 500 | An unknown constraint violation means a broken invariant on the server | **Kept as 500** (logged at ERROR). Mapping it to 409 would hide bugs |
